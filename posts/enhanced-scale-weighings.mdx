## Context

This project involved evolving the desktop scale application into something that could be made mobile and subsequently more guided and usable.

## The problem

Much like the other offerings at AMCS, the mobile scale scenarios are there to address the manual note taking and time lost from either not getting the information quickly or having to physically move from one place to another to get certain information about materials or the people bringing the materials into a weighing yard. Also, the desktop solutions we offered were informationally rich, but due to the nature of the screens they were “tied” to a physical desk.

## Research / discovery

Something that was quite valuable was actually visiting a scale house. From here, we could see that the end user was still relying on written analog or manual methods to make calculations (such as on a calculator) and keep track of information or share with others across the yard. Unique situations can arise when bringing in materials, so it became quite clear that there needed to be some means of sharing notes about certain tickets from one end of the workflow to another. The capabilities and affordances of those in the office versus those (with gloves) in the yard were made clear from visiting and also conversations with customers.

## Design decisions

Through various feedback sessions over the design phase we learned:
* Buttons were made quite large to accommodate gloved hands in the yard.
* Text also had to increase in size, as glare from the sun could make smaller text difficult to read.
* Making the notes prominent on the screen, so that important information can be quickly referenced and not behind unnecessary buttons.

## Collaboration

This project borrowed some mobile interaction concepts from our simpler material and scanning solutions that we had recently finished, so the task was to maintain that consistency with the PM and developers while making a Design System that was suitable for those working with gloves in the yard. The PM reached out to customers to gain insight into which devices they used and in what environments they worked. I was able to leverage this expert knowledge to form initial mockups and collaborate and refine the implementations within the separate PO and developer “pods”.

## Iteration

Initially the content of the tablet scale solution was driven by the desktop solutions. Operators told us they didn't need that much information in the yard. Working with them, we learned their priority was to move quickly to the next vehicle after weighing, leaving additional information to be handled in the office.

## Outcome

The issue with not being able to work concurrently at two different locations was addressed. The smart use of statuses for weighing tickets helped us to identify what needed to be worked on at the right moment. The customer mentioned that although the data was synced, it was a large volume to process in a scale house, where there were many trucks and people entering.
